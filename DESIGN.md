# Eigth Trigrams Furnace — dungeon plan

Premise: the Eight Trigrams Furnace has spilled; its treasures have scattered
and the creatures that seized them are demon lords. Zhong Kui sends mortals
to recover them.

## Rules every dungeon follows

- **One new verb per dungeon.** A mini-boss halfway through guards the
  dungeon's treasure, which gives the player a new way to act on the world.
  It is needed to finish that dungeon and opens places elsewhere.
- **The boss's reward extends something you already carry** (never a new
  button), plus a peach of immortality (+25 maximum health for every player).
- **What makes a dungeon distinct is how you move through it**, not only its
  enemies: each has its own structure (rooms, open ground, a climb, darkness,
  stealth, illusion).
- **The mini-boss teaches what the boss tests.** (The Clever Devil's small
  name-calling before the Silver King's.)
- **Each enemy family forces a different response** and checks a different
  class (the toads leave the archer unchallenged; later families don't).
- **Inexhaustible sources and latching doors** keep every dungeon free of
  soft-locks; the design check proves it for each map.
- Warning colours: red = get out of the way, gold = parryable, purple =
  don't act. Elements: one colour each.

## Order

Reed Marsh first. Then Bamboo Valley, the Flaming Mountains and the Centipede
Caverns in any order; each treasure reveals secrets in the others. The Tomb
City needs the mirror (its entrance is hidden). The Fox Spirit's palace needs
everything. The finale is the Furnace itself.

## 1. Reed Marsh cave (built)

- **Structure:** rooms and doors; carrying elements between rooms.
- **Enemies:** toads (leap red, tongue gold).
- **Mini-boss:** Clever Devil and Wily Worm → **the Purple-Gold Gourd**
  (draw in and pour out water, fire, weakened creatures).
- **Boss:** the Golden and Silver Horned Kings → **the jade vase** (a second
  chamber for the gourd) + peach.
- **Teaches:** the three warning colours; the gourd; posture breaks.

## 2. Bamboo Valley (Red Boy)

- **Structure:** open ground instead of rooms: a valley of bamboo groves and
  ravines with no doors, gated by terrain. **Fire spreads**: a lit grove burns
  outward over a few seconds, clearing paths, destroying rope bridges, and
  endangering whoever lit it. Controlled burns (and water to stop them) are
  the valley's puzzles.
- **Enemies:** snakes. Ambushers hidden in tall grass until close (reward
  cautious movement; burning the grass flushes them), and spitters that keep
  their distance (the first real pressure on the archer).
- **Mini-boss:** a great python in the ravines → **the Diamond Snare**: thrown
  loop that pulls things to you, or you to anchors (swing across ravines,
  drag burning bundles, strip shields).
- **Boss:** Red Boy, in a clearing that he sets alight; his True Samadhi Fire
  spreads faster than normal fire and water only slows it. The snare pulls
  him out of his own flames, where he is weak.
- **Reward:** **Samadhi fire**: fire in the gourd no longer goes out; braziers
  you light stay lit in rain, and it burns lacquered wood ordinary fire
  cannot (used in the Tomb City).

## 3. Flaming Mountains (Princess Iron Fan)

- **Structure:** a climb. Heat zones sap health unless you are in shade or
  cooled (pour water over yourself from the gourd); cool zones are safe
  ground between. Wind currents run along the slopes and carry whatever
  stands in them. A dungeon about routes and timing rather than rooms.
- **Enemies:** scorpions. Armoured in front, vulnerable from the side and
  rear; the sting is a slow gold strike, the claw grab a red one. Rewards
  flanking (the dao) and punishes standing in front (the glaive must use its
  block).
- **Mini-boss:** the Princess's fire-spirit steward → **the palm-leaf fan**:
  a gust that pushes objects and enemies, blows out fires at range, turns
  wind currents, and lets you glide short gaps.
- **Boss:** Princess Iron Fan on a plateau ringed by drops; her fan's gusts
  push players toward the edges. Fanning back cancels her gusts.
- **Reward:** **the Wind-Fixing Pill** (the novel's counter to her fan): you
  can no longer be pushed by wind or knockback when standing still.

## 4. Centipede Caverns (the Hundred-Eyed Demon Lord)

- **Structure:** darkness. Light reaches only a few metres around a lit
  brazier or a carried flame; the rest must be lit to be seen. Deep shafts
  make it vertical: levels linked by ledges and snare anchors.
- **Enemies:** centipedes: long, segmented, armoured on top; they coil
  around a player (red ring closing in) and are vulnerable only at the head
  or when overturned (the kick, the gale).
- **Mini-boss:** a blind cave demon who hunts by sound → **the
  demon-revealing mirror**: reflects light (beams across chambers to light
  and open them) and shows what illusion hides.
- **Boss:** the Hundred-Eyed Demon Lord, whose eyes cast beams of golden
  light that trap whoever they touch; the mirror reflects them back at him.
- **Reward:** **the mirror's second face**: reflected light burns, turning
  every light source into a weapon against things of darkness.

## 5. Tomb City (a jiangshi general)

- **Structure:** stealth. Jiangshi are blind and find the living by breath:
  hold the breath button to go unnoticed (limited, and qi-draining), move
  between tombs, choose which fights to take. The entrance is hidden and only
  the mirror shows it; lacquered tomb doors need Samadhi fire.
- **Enemies:** jiangshi (hopping, unstoppable when awake, sealed by
  talismans: the scholar's day) and geckos on the walls, dropping from above
  (a warning shadow on the ground).
- **Mini-boss:** a Taoist gravekeeper gone astray → **the soul bell**: its
  ring stuns every jiangshi in earshot, commands sealed ones to walk, and
  resonates bronze gongs (sound puzzles).
- **Boss:** the jiangshi general, who raises the dead as the fight goes on;
  the bell turns his raised dead against him.
- **Reward:** **the bell's deep note**: a ring that also breaks enemy
  posture.

## 6. The Fox Spirit's palace (a nine-tailed fox)

- **Structure:** illusion. Corridors loop, doors lead back where you came
  from, walls are false; the mirror shows the true layout. Needs every
  treasure: this is the dungeon that asks the player to combine them.
- **Enemies:** everything met before, in illusory copies that vanish in the
  mirror's light; and the fox's servants.
- **Boss:** the fox, who takes the shape of a party member (in solo, of the
  player). The mirror, and the purple warning, tell the true from the false.
- **Reward:** the last treasures of the spill, and the way to Heaven.

## 7. The Furnace (finale)

The Eight Trigrams Furnace in the Tushita Palace: every verb, every family,
and the answer to who spilled it and why (deliberately left open until the
dungeons exist).
